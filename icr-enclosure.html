PROJECT DEEP-DIVE

ICR Enclosure Project

A clean-sheet redesign of the motorized enclosure that protects fielded equipment, trading a belt-driven dome that needed constant tuning for a self-closing, bolt-together clamshell that is more than 40% lighter.

DESIGN WEIGHT: −40%+ VS. LEGACY UNIT
CHAPTER 01

The Design Challenge

Several of ICR's fielded systems sit inside a motorized enclosure that closes to protect sensitive equipment from rain, wind, and dust. The existing unit did its job, but it was hard to live with. Its belt-driven mechanism had too many moving parts and needed tedious manual tuning, and it was large and heavy enough that shipping it was a project in itself.

My task was to design a replacement from scratch. It needed a simpler mechanism, maintenance a field technician could handle, and a smaller, lighter package to ship. When open, the lids also had to clear the ground and keep out of the equipment's required field of view.

CHAPTER 02

A Simpler Mechanism

The first idea came from an unlikely place: a lunchbox cooler, whose curved lid swings open on one simple pivot. A single full-size lid would need too much clearance, so I split it into two quarter-round halves that open away from each other like a clamshell. Over several rounds of prototypes I moved the pivot and refined the lid path until it met the clearance and field-of-view requirements.

The key move was adding counterweights. With each lid balanced about its pivot, the motor no longer has to hold the lid up; it only has to move it. A gas spring biases the lids closed, a hydraulic damper softens the end of the closing stroke, and a stepper motor with a 10:1 gearbox replaces the belt drive.

CHAPTER 03

Analysis That Shaped the Design

Motion — Taking the Load Off the Motor

I used SolidWorks Motion to simulate the full open, hold, and close cycle and to size the motor. The early concept needed about 100 in-lb of torque just to hold a lid open, so the motor was always working. With the final counterweight and gas-spring arrangement, simulated holding torque at full open dropped to nearly zero. When the lid is closed, the large load that keeps it shut in wind goes into a mechanical hard stop, not the motor.

SIMULATED HOLDING TORQUE, LID OPEN ~100 → ~0 in-lb EARLY CONCEPT → FINAL DESIGN

Wind — Finding the Worst Case

I ran CFD on a simplified model in an 80 mph wind. Wind on the rounded outer surface tends to push the lids closed, which works in the design's favor. The harder case is a "scoop": one lid stuck open with its concave side facing the wind, which tries to pry it farther open. That case set the load for the structural analysis.

Structure — Reading the Model Honestly

I ran FEA on one lid, its supporting members, and its counterweights under that worst-case wind load plus the counterweight mass. Two results needed judgment rather than face value. The model showed the structure bending away from the motor bulkhead, but it leaves out the motor shaft, which stiffens that region in the real assembly. It also showed a strain spike at one corner of the counterweight flange, where the model leaves out the seam joint that reinforces the actual part.

CHAPTER 04

Outcome

DESIGN WEIGHT −40%+ VS. LEGACY ENCLOSURE

The finished design is more than 40% lighter, bolts together without welding in the main frame, and needs almost no motor torque to hold its lids open. It is being fielded across more than 20 systems.

The central decision was to treat actuation, structure, and assembly as one problem: counterbalance the lids, send the closing loads into a hard stop, and build the frame from repeated parts.
